# Who buys, and who we target

Status: EXAMPLE

The agent reads this file before it maps a buying committee. Replace the example with your own offer: run the skill and it asks you the questions, or edit the file yourself and set `Status: yours`.

## What we sell

Evaboot: exports LinkedIn Sales Navigator searches and lists to a spreadsheet or CRM, with verified professional emails, and keeps lead lists fresh with recurring exports.

## Target accounts

- B2B software companies, 51 to 500 employees.
- A sales or business development team of 5 people or more: they prospect on LinkedIn every week.
- Tier 1: a sales team of 15 or more, or a team that grew in the last year. Tier 2: a sales team of 5 to 14. Tier 3: a smaller team, or no sign that they prospect.

## The buying committee

| Role | Who it is here | What they care about |
|---|---|---|
| Decision maker | Head or VP of Sales, CRO, Chief Commercial Officer, CEO or founder under 100 people | Pipeline per rep, cost per meeting, tool budget |
| Champion | Sales Ops, RevOps, Head of SDR or BDR team, Head of Growth | Clean lists, hours saved on list building, CRM data quality |
| Influencer | Marketing Ops, Head of Marketing, Demand Generation | ABM lists, target account coverage, ad audiences |
| User | SDRs, BDRs, Account Executives, Business Developers | Less copy-paste, verified emails, more time to sell |
| Skip | Everyone else: support, recruiting, finance, account managers with no prospecting | |

## How to contact them

- Contact order: start with the champion, then the decision maker with what the champion cares about, then one user. An influencer comes last, and only on a tier 1 account.
- At most 4 people per account. Skip the rest, even when they fit a role.
- Email only when the address is verified (`safe`). Otherwise LinkedIn.
- The angle is one sentence about their own job, from their title and the account facts. Never a guess about the person.
